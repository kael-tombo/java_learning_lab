# EBS Financials — Vision

## Where this lab takes you
From chart of accounts to a working subledger, covering GL, AP, AR, Cash, and
Fixed Assets as one accounting system rather than six separate ones.

## The Arc
1. **Chart of accounts** — structure, segments, and meaning.
2. **GL** — the accounting engine and its journals.
3. **SLA** — how subledger activity becomes GL lines.
4. **AP** — invoices, holds, payment terms, payment execution.
5. **AR** — receipts, applications, aging.
6. **Cash** — bank reconciliation and cash positioning.
7. **FA** — asset register, depreciation, retirements.
8. **Intercompany** — eliminating between entities.

## Milestones (checkable)
- [ ] M1: Design a chart of accounts with a stated segment logic.
- [ ] M2: Enter and post a manual journal and explain its SLA impact.
- [ ] M3: Create an SLA accounting template for AP invoices.
- [ ] M4: Diagnose an AP hold and release it through the correct path.
- [ ] M5: Apply a receipt to an invoice and show the resulting aging change.
- [ ] M6: Reconcile a bank account to GL and explain any difference.
- [ ] M7: Run a depreciation journal and verify asset net book value.
- [ ] M8: Produce an intercompany elimination entry.

## Anti-Goals
- Treating GL, AP, and AR as unrelated systems.
- Posting directly to GL instead of through the subledger.
- Leaving SLA undefined and letting every transaction create a GL line.
- Assuming a manual journal is faster than fixing the source transaction.

## The one-sentence thesis
The subledgers record events and GL records the accounting — if they disagree,
find the SLA definition before you adjust a balance.
