# EBS Manufacturing — Vision

## Where this lab takes you
From a bill of material and routing to scheduled production on a shop floor —
understanding why discrete, repetitive, and lot-based manufacturing behave
differently under the same planning engine.

## The Arc
1. **Product structure** — BOM, routing, work centres, operations.
2. **Discrete** — WIP jobs, material issues, completions.
3. **Repetitive** — backflush and yield accounting.
4. **Lot-based** — traceability, genealogy, lot attributes.
5. **Engineering change** — ECOs and ECNs with effective dates.
6. **Planning** — MPS, MRP, and DRP; what each is for.
7. **Capacity** — CRP, constraints, and the honest question of throughput.
8. **Quality** — inspections, results, and genealogy impact.

## Milestones (checkable)
- [ ] M1: Build a BOM with at least 3 levels and a routing with 4 operations.
- [ ] M2: Release and execute a discrete WIP job, issuing and completing material.
- [ ] M3: Explain backflush and run one repetitive job with it.
- [ ] M4: Trace lot genealogy from raw material to finished good.
- [ ] M5: Effect an ECO/ECN and show which orders use which revision.
- [ ] M6: Run MPS/MRP and justify the planned order quantity.
- [ ] M7: Load a work centre and read its capacity implication.
- [ ] M8: Run a quality inspection and show the genealogy consequence.

## Anti-Goals
- Planning a BOM without a routing and expecting the schedule to hold.
- Treating lot traceability as optional for regulated industries.
- Running MRP without cleaning up past-due demand first.
- Ignoring the work-centre bottleneck when explaining schedule slippage.

## The one-sentence thesis
A schedule is only as good as the routing and the capacity behind it — MRP
without constraints is a wish list with dates attached.
