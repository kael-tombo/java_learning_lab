# Why Causal Inference Exists

Decisions are interventions: approve the drug, ship the feature, raise
the wage. Data shows what happened under past choices, but the decision
needs what would happen under a new one - a question about potential
outcomes, not about history's correlations.

Naive comparisons answer the wrong question. Sicker patients get the
stronger drug, motivated users try the new feature, booming states
raise wages - so treated-vs-untreated gaps mix the effect with the
selection that assigned treatment. Acting on them mistreats, mis-ships,
mis-regulates.

Causal inference exists to close that gap honestly. Randomization
manufactures the missing counterfactual by construction; matching,
differencing, cutoffs, and instruments reconstruct it under named
assumptions when randomization is impossible. The field's real product
is not a method but a discipline: no causal sentence without the design
and the assumption that earned it.

Without it, organizations optimize mirages - rewarding hospitals for
healthy patients, features for eager users, policies for lucky timing.

## A concrete scene

A job-training program shows higher earnings for graduates. The mayor
wants expansion; the auditor asks who enrolled. Answer: motivated
workers with prior experience - the program creamed. Naive comparison
credits the program for the motivation it selected, and expansion buys
cream-skimming at scale.

## What design changes

Randomized slots (lottery) balance motivation in expectation; the mean
gap becomes the effect. Where lotteries are impossible, stratifying by
prior earnings plus a sensitivity note bounds the self-deception. Either
way the decision rests on design, not on hope wearing a spreadsheet.

## The alternative, priced

Cities have bought useless programs and scrapped useful ones on pooled
numbers - each error measured in budgets and livelihoods. One assumption
sentence per claim is the cheapest insurance in public policy.
