# Mental Models - Causal Inference

## 1. The missing half

Every unit hides one half of its story (the road not taken). Methods
are just disciplined ways of borrowing someone else's half - and the
borrowing terms are the assumptions.

## 2. Randomization as a coin that erases history

The coin does not care about severity, wealth, or motivation, so both
arms inherit equal shares of everything - measured or not. That is the
whole business model of a trial.

## 3. Confounding as a stage magician

The magician (severity) stands behind both the treatment choice and the
recovery, making the drug look guilty. Stratification walks around the
stage to see the trick from the side.

## 4. Pooling as a blender

Blending strata with different mixes produces a flavor (rate) neither
stratum has. Simpson's paradox is the blender's signature drink - never
taste pooled numbers without checking the ingredients.

## 5. Parallel trends as a dance partner

DiD assumes the control group dances the treated group's counterfactual
steps. Pre-period footage (parallel history) suggests they know the
same moves; diverging footage ends the show.

## 6. The cutoff as a knife edge

Near a threshold, assignment is nearly luck - units a point apart are
twins separated by bureaucracy. Effects found there are local to the
edge, not passports to far-away populations.

## 7. Assumptions as licenses

Each method carries a license plate (no-unmeasured-confounding,
parallel-trends, exclusion). Driving the conclusion without the plate
visible is the offense this lab trains you never to commit.

## 8. Sensitivity as a stress test

Every causal bridge posts a load limit: the confounder strength that
collapses it. Sensitivity analysis drives heavier trucks until the crack
shows - publish the limit with the bridge, always.

## 9. The second design as a second witness

One witness (matching) may lie for its own reasons; DiD lies for
different ones. Agreement between designs with different assumptions is
worth more than any single p-value - call the second witness.
