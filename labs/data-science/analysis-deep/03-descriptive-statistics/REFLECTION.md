# Descriptive Statistics - Reflection

## Prompts

1. Mean 10.57 vs median 9.0: write the one-line summary for a capacity plan
   and the different one-line summary for a revenue-total forecast.
2. A vendor reports "variance 30.8" on what you suspect is a sample of
   thousands. What do you ask, and how much does the answer move intervals?
3. Skew +0.58, kurtosis -0.62, IQR 6.5, sd 6.00: assemble the two-sentence
   shape story for a newcomer to this column.
4. r = 0.99 on n = 5: a teammate wants it in the headline. Draft the reply
   that keeps the number but adds the missing cautions.

## Self-check sketches

1. Capacity: "plan for 9 typical, with tail to 21." Revenue: "use mean 10.57
   times volume - totals need the mean, and the tail counts there."
2. Ask "divisor n or n - 1, and n = ?". At large n the factor (n-1)/n
   barely matters; at n = 7 it shifts variance 30.82 to 35.95 (~17%).
3. "Mildly right-skewed bulk around 7-13 with a stretch to 21; tails
   otherwise calm, so median/IQR describe it and normal alarms would
   over-fire."
4. "Keep 0.99 with n = 5 stated, association-only wording, and the scatter
   attached - five points cannot carry a headline alone."

## Habits worth keeping

- Always print the pair (mean with median, sd with IQR, r with n).
- Label every variance with its divisor at the point of reporting.
- Keep the seven-number dataset as a pocket calculator check for any new
  summary routine you write.

## One more prompt

5. A dashboard shows only "avg 10.6, stdev 6.0" for this column. List the
three most misleading readings a hurried executive could take, and the
single added line that prevents all three. (Sketch: typical=10.6; spread is
symmetric noise; extremes unlikely. Prevention: "median 9.0, IQR 6.5, max
21, n = 7 - right-skewed, tail matters.")
