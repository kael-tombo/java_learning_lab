# Descriptive Statistics - Mental Models

## 1. Center as three witnesses

Mean (every value votes, wealthiest votes count most), median (the middle
voter), mode (the most common ballot). On {3..21} they testify 10.57, 9.0,
7.0 - disagreement among witnesses is the finding, pointing at the tail.

## 2. n vs n - 1 as degrees of freedom

Fitting the mean spends one degree of freedom: residuals about x-bar have
only n - 1 independent values. Dividing by n - 1 pays for what was spent.
Describing the seven numbers needs no payment (n); generalizing beyond them
does (n - 1).

## 3. sd as typical surprise

Population sd 5.55: a typical value sits about 5.5 from the mean. If a new
observation lands 20 away, that is nearly 4 sd - surprising under any
bell-shaped reading, routine under a heavy tail. The sd sets the surprise
unit; kurtosis warns when the unit misleads.

## 4. Quartiles as the honest five

Min 3, Q1 7, median 9, Q3 13.5, max 21: five numbers, no formulas beyond
halving, robust to anything short of majority contamination. When mean/sd
and five-number summaries disagree, believe the five and investigate.

## 5. Correlation as standardized co-movement

Covariance 5.0 carries units (x-units times y-units); dividing by the two
spreads strips units, leaving r = 0.99: x and y move together almost
perfectly linearly. Standardization is what makes 0.99 comparable across
datasets - and what tempts the causation leap. Resist it.

## 6. Moments as a ladder

Mean (1st), variance (2nd), skew (3rd), kurtosis (4th): each higher rung is
more informative and more fragile. Climb only as far as the data's
cleanliness supports; on dirty data, stop at quartiles.

## 7. Summaries as witnesses in order

Call center first (what is typical?), spread second (how far from typical?),
quartiles third (who resists the tail?), shape fourth (which way does it
lean?), association last (what moves together?). Skipping the order - r
before quartiles, moments before medians - is how fragile numbers testify
first and set the story wrongly.
