# Mental Models - Experimentation

## 1. The fair coin as judge

Randomization is the only judge both teams trust: it gives each arm
equal shares of eager users, grumpy users, bots, and Mondays. Argue
with the verdict by rerunning, never by re-slicing.

## 2. The interval as the honest sentence

"d = +1.2pp" is a boast; "[+0.28, +2.12]pp" is a report. The width is
the experiment telling you how much it knows - listen to it.

## 3. Error budget as cash

Alpha 0.05 is a wallet, not a ritual: every peek and every extra metric
spends from it. Fixed horizons and pre-registration are just not
spending twice.

## 4. SRM as the broken scale

Weighing teams on a rigged scale, precisely, is worse than not weighing.
The split check comes before the readout the way calibration comes
before measurement.

## 5. Novelty as sugar rush

New UI is candy: week-one energy, week-four crash. Only effects that
survive the crash are nutrition - plan the horizon past dessert.

## 6. Power as a telescope

Sample size is aperture: small scopes see only bright (large) effects.
Demanding a dim detection from a small scope wastes everyone's nights -
size the glass to the target first.

## 7. The log as compound interest

Each logged test (hypothesis, MDE, result, decision) pays interest on
all future tests. Teams without logs re-pay the same tuition quarterly.

## 8. The horizon as a contract

Fixed horizon is a promise to your future self: "I will not decide until
Friday." Peeking breaks the promise the way opening the oven breaks the
cake - the looking ruins the thing looked at.

## 9. Guardrails as smoke detectors

Goals measure the cooking; guardrails smell smoke. Nobody optimizes the
meal by disabling the detector - veto-grade regressions end the test no
matter how good dinner smells.
