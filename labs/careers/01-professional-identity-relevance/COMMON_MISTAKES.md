# Professional Identity — Common Mistakes

Six recurring identity failures. Each is falsifiable: you can check your own
materials for it in under a minute.

## 1. Capability tourism

Listing every technology you ever touched, none owned. The tell: verbs are
all "used", "worked with", "exposed to" — no artifact, no outcome, no owner.
**Correction**: pick the three claims you can defend in a 20-minute interview
grilling and cut the rest. A short list you can defend beats a long list
you can only recite.

## 2. Expert inflation

"Expert" appearing more than once in a skills section. Experts in a field
rarely label themselves experts; they name artifacts. **Correction**: graded
vocabulary (fluent / comfortable / familiar / exposed-to) plus one evidence
row per claim. The row does the ranking so the adjective does not have to.

## 3. Availability positioning

"Seeking new opportunities in a growth-oriented environment." It answers a
question nobody asked ("are you looking?") and never answers the one they
did ("what do you do?"). **Correction**: open with function and domain;
availability is implied by the fact you applied, and belongs in the cover
note at most.

## 4. Title-only identity

Relying on company-local titles ("MTS II", "Lead", "Tech Lead 3") as if they
transferred. **Correction**: function first — what you built, operated,
tested, or prevented — title second, only if it adds information.

## 5. The fossil profile

A self-description last maintained years ago, still claiming technologies
you no longer touch. It tells careful readers your self-knowledge has a
multi-year lag. **Correction**: the quarterly audit — reread, delete stale,
reprice survivors; date anything historical ("2019–2022").

## 6. Underselling as humility

Refusing all specificity to seem modest ("I've been lucky to work on some
cool stuff"). Underselling is as dishonest as overclaiming — it hides real
artifacts and makes the reader dig. **Correction**: honest scoping in both
directions: full price on what you own, graded discount on what you do not.
Humility is about tone, never about deleting evidence.

## 7. Audience-free positioning

One generic summary deployed against every job. The relevance term goes to
zero for every specific reader. **Correction**: keep the claim set fixed,
rewrite the relevance mapping per target — two or three positioning variants,
not fifty (you drift into fiction past three).
