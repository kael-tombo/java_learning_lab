# Professional Identity — Flashcards

Twelve cards. Cover the answer, answer from memory, then check the exact
claim — these are falsifiable statements, not vibes.

## Card 1
**Q**: Every identity claim is priced in two currencies. Name them.
**A**: Evidence (can a skeptical reader verify it?) and relevance (does the
reader's current need discount it?).

## Card 2
**Q**: Why is "four years" stronger than "experienced"?
**A**: Numbers are auditable; adjectives require the reader to extend trust
you have not yet earned.

## Card 3
**Q**: What is the middle term in claim → ___ → hired?
**A**: Relevance — the real mapping between what you did and the target
team's constraint set. Missing relevance reads as capability tourism.

## Card 4
**Q**: What evidence unit does a [Junior] identity price on? A [Senior]?
**A**: Junior: execution — shipped work, isolated bugs, learning pace.
Senior: judgment and ownership — trade-offs made and owned, systems kept
alive, decisions defended.

## Card 5
**Q**: Title vs function — which crosses company borders, and what order?
**A**: Function crosses borders; titles are local tokens. State function
first; add the title only when it adds information.

## Card 6
**Q**: What is the trust ledger's red-ink effect?
**A**: One overclaim discounts neighboring true rows — an "expert" in four
frameworks who shipped one is discounted on all four.

## Card 7
**Q**: The corrective reflex for overclaiming is ___.
**A**: Graded vocabulary: fluent, comfortable, familiar, exposed-to — cheap
words that survive follow-up questions.

## Card 8
**Q**: What is identity decay and its maintenance ritual?
**A**: Claims rot when you stop shipping what they describe; the ritual is a
quarterly audit — reread, delete stale, reprice survivors.

## Card 9
**Q**: The one-liner structure that survives hiring rooms?
**A**: [level/function] + [domain] + [artifact proof], delivered flat, with
no adjectives requiring unearned trust.

## Card 10
**Q**: Positioning is choosing ___ before choosing ___.
**A**: Your audience before your adjectives.

## Card 11
**Q**: A skill you have not shipped in years should be ___, not deleted.
**A**: Downgraded and dated ("2019–2022, rusty, will re-ramp") — graded
history generates interview stories; deletion generates nothing.

## Card 12
**Q**: When is a summary sentence "availability positioning", and why is it
weak?
**A**: "Seeking opportunities in a growth-oriented environment" — it frames
you as available rather than needed, and prices zero on both currencies.
