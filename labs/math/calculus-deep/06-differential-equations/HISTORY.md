# History: Differential Equations

## First classifications (1670s-1696)
Newton classified first-order equations in a 1670s manuscript on
fluxions. Leibniz solved the separable cases; Jacob Bernoulli posed
his equation in 1695 and Leibniz solved it in 1696. Daniel Bernoulli
solved the Riccati equation, posed by Jacopo Riccati in 1724, by
reduction to Bernoulli form.

## Euler systematizes (1730s-1768)
Euler treated integrating factors in the 1730s and gathered the
subject in his Institutiones calculi integralis (1768), which also
contains the polygonal method now called Euler's method. Laplace's
transform techniques for linear equations date from the late 18th
century.

## Growth models (1838-1845)
Pierre-Francois Verhulst introduced the logistic equation in memoirs
of 1838 and 1845, modeling population saturation against
Malthusian exponential growth. The lab's logistic stability sketch
descends directly from his analysis.

## Existence and uniqueness (1890-1894)
Emile Picard published successive approximations in 1890; Ernst
Lindelof refined the theorem in 1894. From then on the existence
hypotheses this lab states (continuity, Lipschitz) were theorems
with names attached.

## Higher-order stepping (1895-1901)
Carl Runge published the first higher-order one-step method in 1895;
Karl Heun and Wilhelm Kutta extended the family around 1900-1901.
The classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method is Kutta's.

## What this lab inherits
Separable, linear, and logistic equations solved and verified by
substitution, Euler steps with measured error, stability sketched:
the 1768 toolkit checked against the 1890s standards.
