# References: Combinatorics

## Primary Sources

- **Blaise Pascal, *Traité du triangle arithmétique* (1665, written 1654)** — the triangle and its combinatorial identities (with Fermat's earlier letters on the problem of points, published in *Tacet*, 1654).
- **Leonhard Euler, letter to Christian Goldbach, 1751** — first treatment of the polygon-triangulation (Catalan) recurrence; published in *Nova Acta Academiae* (1761). Euler's paper on the number of distinct permutations with repetitions ("calcul des arrangements") also dates from the 1750s.
- **James Stirling, *Methodus Differentialis* (1730)** — Stirling numbers, factorial asymptotics, and early approximations of sums.
- **August Ferdinand Möbius, "Über eine neue Anwendung der Determinanten" (1832)** — the function μ(n) encoding the signs of the inclusion–exclusion sieve.
- **Frank P. Ramsey, "On a problem of formal logic" (1930)** — the theorem founding Ramsey theory.
- **George Pólya, "Kombinatorische Anzahlbestimmungen für Gruppen, Graphen und chemische Verbindungen" (1937)** — enumeration up to symmetry.

## Textbooks

- **Richard P. Stanley, *Enumerative Combinatorics*, Volume 1, 2nd ed. (Cambridge, 2011)** and **Volume 2 (Cambridge, 2021)**. The graduate standard; Ch. 1 (the twelvefold way), Ch. 2 (generating functions), and Ch. 3 (the sieve) are the core references for this lab.
- **Richard P. Stanley, *Catalan Numbers* (Cambridge, 2015)** — the modern monograph: 66 meanings of Cₙ including triangulations, Dyck paths, binary trees, and non-crossing partitions.
- **Miklós Bóna, *A Walk Through Combinatorics*, 3rd ed. (World Scientific, 2016)**. Undergraduate entry point with excellent problem sets; covers bijections, recurrences, and the sieve gently.
- **Ronald Graham, Donald Knuth, Oren Patashnik, *Concrete Mathematics*, 2nd ed. (Addison-Wesley, 1994)**. Sums, recurrences, binomial identities, and asymptotics written for computer scientists — Ch. 5 (binomial coefficients) is the direct theory behind the Pascal-triangle work here.
- **Jacobus van Lint & Richard Wilson, *A Course in Combinatorics*, 2nd ed. (Cambridge, 2001)**. Charming coverage of designs, codes, and graph-counting.
- **Peter Cameron, *Combinatorics: Topics, Techniques, Algorithms*, 2nd ed. (Cambridge, 1994)**. Bridges theory and algorithmic generation.

## Algorithms

- **Donald Knuth, *The Art of Computer Programming*, Vol. 1, 3rd ed. (1997), §1.2.5–1.2.6** (combinatorial sums, binomial coefficients) and **Vol. 4A (2011), §7.2.1** (generation of permutations and combinations; Heap's algorithm and next-combination lexicographic generation).
- **Robert Sedgewick & Kevin Wayne, *Algorithms*, 4th ed. (Addison-Wesley, 2011), Ch. 2 and §1.4** — combinatorics as the basis of data-structure analysis (permutations, binary search trees as Catalan objects).

## History

- **John Stillwell, *Mathematics and Its History*, 3rd ed. (Springer, 2010)** — reliable attribution for Pascal's triangle (earlier in China and Persia), Euler's triangulation problem, and the development of enumeration.
- **Donald Knuth, *The Art of Computer Programming*, Vol. 1, 3rd ed. (1997), §1.2.6** — historical notes on binomial coefficients across cultures, with primary references.
- **Herbert S. Wilf, *generatingfunctionology*, 2nd ed. (Academic Press, 1994)** — free from the author's page; the bridge from counting to lab 05, with the twelvefold way and the coefficient-extraction viewpoint.
