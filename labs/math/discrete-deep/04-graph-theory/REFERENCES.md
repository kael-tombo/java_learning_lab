# References: Graph Theory

## Primary Sources

- **Leonhard Euler, "Solutio problematis ad geometriam situs pertinentis" (1736)**, *Commentarii academiae scientiarum imperialis Petropolitanae* 8:128–140. The Königsberg bridges paper — the founding document of graph theory (English translations widely available, e.g., in *The Kinds of Infinity* anthologies and Chartrand–Zhang's *Introduction to Graph Theory*).
- **Arthur Cayley, "On the theory of analytical forms called trees" (1857)**, *Philosophical Magazine* 13:172–176 — the nⁿ⁻² counting of labeled trees.
- **Philip Hall, "On representatives of subsets" (1935)**, *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* 10:26–30 — Hall's marriage theorem.
- **Edsger W. Dijkstra, "A note on two problems in connexion with graphs" (1959)**, *Numerische Mathematik* 1:269–271 — the two-page shortest-path paper.
- **Lester R. Ford Jr. & Delbert R. Fulkerson, *Flows in Networks* (Princeton University Press, 1962)** — max-flow, min-cut, and the primal-dual method.

## Textbooks

- **Reinhard Diestel, *Graph Theory*, 5th ed. (Springer, 2017)** — the standard graduate text; free electronic edition from the author's site. Chapters on connectivity, flows, and coloring are the canonical treatment.
- **Douglas West, *Introduction to Graph Theory*, 2nd ed. (Prentice Hall, 2011)** — the standard undergraduate reference with excellent exercises; Ch. 1–7 cover everything in this lab.
- **Béla Bollobás, *Modern Graph Theory*, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 184 (Springer, 1998)** — concise and advanced; strong on extremal and probabilistic topics.
- **John Bondy & U. S. R. Murty, *Graph Theory*, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 244 (Springer, 2008)** — comprehensive with historical notes.
- **Lovász, *Problems and Exercises in Combinatorics and Graph Theory*** — the standard problem collection for drilling proofs.

## Algorithms

- **Thomas Cormen, Charles Leiserson, Ronald Rivest, Clifford Stein, *Introduction to Algorithms* (CLRS), 4th ed. (MIT Press, 2022), Ch. 22–26** — BFS/DFS, shortest paths (Dijkstra 24.3, Bellman–Ford 24.1), MST (21–23), max-flow (Ch. 26), with correctness proofs and complexity analysis.
- **Robert Sedgewick & Kevin Wayne, *Algorithms*, 4th ed. (Addison-Wesley, 2011), Ch. 4** — graph implementations in practice, including the `Graph` interface style used in lab 04's architecture.
- **Robert Tarjan, "Depth-first search and linear graph algorithms" (SIAM J. Computing, 1972)** — DFS as the basis for connectivity, topological sort, and strong components.
- **Jon Kleinberg & Éva Tardos, *Algorithm Design*, Ch. 3 (network flow) and Ch. 6 (graph problems)** — problem-driven exposition with approximation and reduction examples.

## Algebraic and Combinatorial Graph Theory

- **Chris Godsil & Gordon Royle, *Algebraic Graph Theory*, GTM 207 (Springer, 2001)** — adjacency/Laplacian spectra, the matrix–tree theorem, and counting via eigenvalues.
- **Frank Harary, *Graph Theory* (Addison-Wesley, 1969)** — the classic that standardized notation (γ, κ, χ).

## Four Color Theorem Proofs

- **Kenneth Appel & Wolfgang Haken, "Every planar map is four colorable" (Illinois J. Math. 21, 1977)** — the 1976 computer-assisted proof.
- **Neil Robertson, Paul Seymour & Robin Thomas, "Every planar map is four colorable" (J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 70, 1997)** — the improved, more verifiable computer proof.
