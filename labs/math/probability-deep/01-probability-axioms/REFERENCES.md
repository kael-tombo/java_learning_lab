# References: Probability Axioms

## Primary sources
- **A. N. Kolmogorov**, *Grundbegriffe der Wahrscheinlichkeitsrechnung*, Springer, 1933. English transl. *Foundations of the Theory of Probability*, Chelsea, 1950 — the original axiomatic treatment; short (60+ pages) and still readable.
- **T. Bayes**, "An Essay towards solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances", communicated by **R. Price**, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society* 53:370–418, 1763 — the inverse-probability paper.
- **J. Bernoulli**, *Ars Conjectandi*, Basel, 1713 (posth.) — weak law of large numbers and early inclusion–exclusion.
- **P.-S. Laplace**, *Théorie analytique des probabilités*, Courcier, 1812 — rule of succession, generating functions.

## Textbooks
- **E. T. Jaynes**, *Probability Theory: The Logic of Science*, Cambridge UP, 2003 — treats probability as extended logic; ch. 1–3 for the axioms, ch. 4 for the Bertrand paradox.
- **W. Feller**, *An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications*, vol. 1, Wiley, 2nd ed. 1968 — the classical source for combinatorial identities and infinite probability.
- **G. R. Grimmett & D. R. Stirzaker**, *Probability and Random Processes*, Oxford, 1982; 3rd ed. 2001 — rigorous treatment of measure and conditional expectation.
- **S. Ross**, *A First Course in Probability*, Pearson, 1st ed. 1976; 10th ed. 2019 — best problem bank for inclusion–exclusion and Bayes.
- **J. K. Blitzstein & J. Hwang**, *Introduction to Probability*, CRC, 2014 — conditioning-first pedagogy; excellent two-table method (same one used in STEP_BY_STEP.md).
- **M. H. DeGroot & M. J. Schervish**, *Probability and Statistics*, 4th ed., Pearson, 2012 — axioms through statistical inference.

## Interpretations and foundations
- **B. de Finetti**, *Theory of Probability*, Wiley, 1974 (1970 IT) — subjectivist position and exchangeability.
- **L. J. Savage**, *The Foundations of Statistics*, Wiley, 1954 — personal probability from preference axioms.
- **R. T. Cox**, "Probability, the frequency theory, and objective truth", *American Journal of Physics* 14:1–13, 1946 — plausibility algebra forces the probability rules.
- **P. R. Halmos**, *Measure Theory*, Van Nostrand, 1950 — the σ-algebra machinery Kolmogorov's axioms presuppose.

## Historical survey
- **T. M. Porter**, *The Rise of Statistical Thinking 1820–1900*, Princeton UP, 1986 — how Laplace, Poisson and Chebyshev moved probability from games to social science.
- **O. Sheynin**, *P. L. Chebyshev's Works on the Theory of Probability*, Nauka, 1962 — on the 1867 inequality.

## Further reading

- **R. B. Ash**, *Basic Probability Theory*, Wiley, 1970 — the shortest rigorous path from axioms to conditional expectation; excellent exercises on conditioning trees.
- **D. J. C. MacKay**, *Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms*, Cambridge UP, 2003, ch. 2–4 — probability as reasoning under uncertainty, with the coding-theoretic justification for priors.
- **J. Pearl**, *Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems*, Morgan Kaufmann, 1988 — conditional independence, d-separation and graphical models; the bridge from these axioms to lab 08's hierarchical models.
- **A. Papoulis**, *Probability, Random Variables, and Stochastic Processes*, 3rd ed., McGraw-Hill, 1991 — engineering-oriented treatment of the same axioms with signal-processing examples.
