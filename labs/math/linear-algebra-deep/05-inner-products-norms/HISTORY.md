# History: Inner Products and Norms

## Geometric roots
Length and angle inequalities begin as geometry: Euclid's Elements
already relates side lengths to angles, and the Pythagorean relation
is the norm identity behind everything in this lab. The modern form
took two thousand years to extract from the picture.

## The inequality (1821-1888)
Cauchy published the finite-sum form of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
in his 1821 Cours d'analyse. Viktor Bunyakovsky extended it to
integrals in 1859; Hermann Amandus Schwarz gave the general inner
product form in 1888. Three names, three settings, one inequality.

## Orthogonalization (1883-1907)
Gram's 1883 paper and Schmidt's 1907 paper supplied the constructive
procedure this lab runs with exact fractions: subtract projections,
normalize, repeat. Schmidt's setting was function spaces, which is why
the algorithm immediately applied beyond R^n.

## Function spaces and Hilbert space (1900s-1930s)
Hilbert's integral-equation school worked with l^2 and L^2 inner
products in the first decade of the 20th century. Von Neumann's
axiomatization around 1929 made abstract Hilbert space the setting of
quantum mechanics; characterizations of inner-product norms by the
parallelogram law followed in the 1930s.

## Orthogonal matrices
Rotation and reflection matrices with Q^T Q = I descend from Euler's
18th-century rigid-body work through Rodrigues' 1840 rotation formula
to Householder's 1958 reflections. Orthogonality as numerical virtue,
not just geometric fact, is a 20th-century insight.

## What this lab inherits
The Cauchy-Schwarz check 9 <= 28, the Gram-Schmidt triple with norms
2, 3/2, 4/3, and the projection formula are 19th-century mathematics
executed with 21st-century exact arithmetic.
