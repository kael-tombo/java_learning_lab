# History: Eigenvalues, Advanced

## Vibrations and principal axes (1829-1846)
Augustin-Louis Cauchy's 1829 memoir on quadratic surfaces proved that
a real symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues with orthogonal axes.
Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi's 1846 paper introduced iterative plane
rotations to diagonalize symmetric matrices, the oldest eigenvalue
algorithm still recognizable today.

## Invariants and canonical form (1858-1878)
Cayley stated the theorem now bearing his and Hamilton's names in
1858 for small cases; Ferdinand Georg Frobenius supplied the general
proof in 1878. Karl Weierstrass's 1868 theory of elementary divisors
and Camille Jordan's 1870 Traite classified even the defective,
non-diagonalizable case that this lab exhibits with its 2-by-2 Jordan
block.

## Continuous origins (1836-1837)
Sturm-Liouville theory of 1836-37, on vibrating strings and heat
conduction, produced infinite-dimensional eigenvalue problems first.
The finite-dimensional spectral theorem descends from that analytic
line through Hilbert's and Schmidt's integral-equation work in the
first decade of the 20th century.

## Iteration era (1929-1961)
Richard von Mises and Hilda Pollaczek-Geiringer published the power
iteration analysis in 1929. The QR algorithm of Francis and
Kublanovskaya (1961) then became the standard dense eigensolver,
usually after preliminary reduction to Hessenberg or tridiagonal form.

## What this lab inherits
The worked polynomial t^2 - 7 t + 10, the spectral decomposition of
the symmetric 2-by-2, and the defective block rehearse the full arc:
generic diagonalizable behavior, guaranteed symmetric behavior, and
the Jordan exception that forced the 1868-1870 classification.
