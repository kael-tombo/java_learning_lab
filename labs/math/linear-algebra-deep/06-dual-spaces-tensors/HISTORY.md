# History: Dual Spaces and Tensors

## Grassmann's products (1844)
Grassmann's 1844 Ausdehnungslehre already contained exterior products,
the alternating special case of the tensor product. The general
product of multilinear maps took the rest of the century to separate
from coordinates.

## The word "tensor" (1898-1900)
Woldemar Voigt introduced the word tensor in 1898 for stress-like
quantities in crystal physics. Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro and Tullio
Levi-Civita then published their absolute differential calculus in
1900, giving the index machinery; Einstein's adoption of it for
general relativity in 1915-16 made tensors standard equipment.

## Kronecker's namesake product
The Kronecker product is named for Leopold Kronecker, whose 19th
century work on bilinear forms and determinants underlies it. The
mixed-product identity this lab verifies, (A x B)(C x D) ordering
preserved under multiplication, is the reason the construction
survives: it linearizes matrix equations without expanding them.

## Duals and bras (1920s-1939)
Dual spaces of normed spaces appear in Banach's 1932 book. Paul Dirac's
1930 Principles of Quantum Mechanics introduced bra and ket vectors;
he published the bra-ket notation itself in 1939, which is dual-space
pairing written as physics.

## Rank-one matrices
Writing a matrix as a sum of outer products is as old as coordinates;
its systematic use for updates (Sherman-Morrison, 1949-50) and for
low-rank approximation belongs to mid-20th-century numerical analysis.

## What this lab inherits
The explicit dual basis phi1 = (x + y) / 2, phi2 = (x - y) / 2, the
Kronecker vector (3, -1, 6, -2), and the mixed-product check
(18, 6, 12, 4) are small instances of machinery built between 1844
and 1939.
